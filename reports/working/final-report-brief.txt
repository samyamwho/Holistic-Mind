Assessment Information
Assessment
Task (with
genre/type)
Final Report
This is the culminating written output, a comprehensive document detailing the entire
project journey. It includes a refined problem statement and literature review (i.e.,
covered in the interim project proposal). However, the focus should be on providing
a thorough account of the design and development of the artefact, a detailed
description of the implementation process, and a robust critical evaluation of the
artefact's relevance and impact within a suitable societal, industrial, or scholarly
context.
Assessment
Title
Individual Hons Project (FYP): <use your Project Title, as the assessment
title>
Things to
include:
Final Report
- Artefact Design: this should be objective and factual (use tables, graphs, and
figures where appropriate).
- Implementation: This is where you explain how your artefact addresses your
project questions. Discuss the construction of the artefact. which can include
structuring and validating the artefact through logical consistency with
appropriate evidence.
- Critical Evaluation: Summarise your artefact and explain its significance. How
does it compare with other similar work from the knowledge base you
reviewed? What is the impact of your artefact? What are the limitations of your
work? Restate the contribution your project has made. Include practical
implications or suggest future research directions.

Use of Artificial Intelligence
PROBHIBITED
You MUST NOT use any generative Artificial Intelligence in this assessment unless specifically
authorised for reasonable adjustments. You MAY use non-generative tools such as a spell-check,
basic grammar check (non-generative), calculator or similar. If you have any doubts about using an
AI tool, please consult the Student AI Guidelines or ask your tutor.
Whilst AI tools can be helpful in assisting learning, when it comes to assessment, the Academic
Misconduct Procedure is clear that this should be a student’s own original work and not the work of
other people or AI tools.
The Use of AI Tools – Student Guidelines follows the same guidelines your lecturers use. If you are
unsure of whether AI is appropriate within your work, please read the guidelines or ask your lecturer.
For advice and guidance around academic writing, please visit the Centre for Academic Success.
Academic Integrity Guidance
Academic Integrity means completing and submitting your own original work while properly
acknowledging sources. Following academic conventions, such as referencing and citation, helps
avoid plagiarism. The Centre for Academic Success offers resources on Moodle, workshops and 1-2-1
support to improve skills like paraphrasing and referencing.
Academic Misconduct
Academic misconduct is conduct that has or may have the effect of providing you with an unfair
advantage by relying on dishonest means to gain an advantage and which therefore compromises your
academic integrity.
The Academic Misconduct Procedure sets out the process we will follow and the penalties we may
apply in cases where we believe you may have compromised your academic integrity by committing
academic misconduct.

The word count refers to everything in the main body of the text (including headings, tables, citations,
